Learn on a Bitaxe. Defend your wealth with real hashrate.

Two companies build 58% of Bitcoin's block templates, so they decide which payments get confirmed. Taking that majority away takes about 164 EH/s of new hashrate on its own templates. That means kilowatt machines in a lot of homes, heating them or running on surplus solar. A Bitaxe is the best classroom in mining, but counted as defense it's a trinket.

This isn't charity. If those builders are told to leave payments out, the payments left out are yours. Many industrial miners sell what they mine to cover power and debt, and they answer to compliance departments. You're the one holding the bitcoin.

The case for home hashrate

  1. Pools pick the payments

    Miners do the hashing, but whoever builds the block template chooses which transactions go into the block. For most of the network, that's the pool. Running a node checks the rules; it doesn't choose what goes in.

  2. Two companies pick for most blocks

    Foundry and the AntPool group build 58% of templates. If they're told to leave a payment out, it waits for someone else's block. Your bitcoin is in those payments.

  3. Home miners can take it back

    Build your own templates with DATUM, Stratum V2 job declaration or solo mining on your own node, and enough of you break that majority.

  4. It takes kilowatts, not trinkets

    That's about 164 EH/s: 821k homes with an S21, or 137M with a Bitaxe. Heat and solar make the big machines worth running.

Bitaxes

Credit where it's due. skot (Skot9000) reverse-engineered Bitmain's mining chips and published the Bitaxe as open-source hardware. Every trace on the board and every line of firmware is public, and the OSMU community keeps building on it. There's no better way to learn how mining works.

But a classroom isn't a fleet. A Bitaxe draws about 18 W, so it can't heat a room, and mining solo it finds a block about once every 17,000 years. It isn't even the cheap option: a used S19j Pro sells for about $185 on eBay, roughly the price of a tabletop miner, and does 104 TH/s instead of 1 to 6.

Geography

Home mining protects against governments as well as pools, but only if it's spread out. China's 2021 ban cut network hashrate by 39% in two months. The US already holds the most hashrate, so a home fleet that's mostly American adds to that risk.

Who builds the blocks

A block's coinbase tag says which pool got paid. It doesn't say who wrote the block template, the list of transactions miners are told to hash. Researcher b10c found strong evidence that a group of pools relay AntPool's templates under their own names. Group the blocks by who actually builds them and three entities become two.

Mining setups

Who writes the template?

The pool's brand doesn't matter. What matters is whose node chooses the transactions your hash works on. Most industrial hash is paid FPPS, which leaves the template with the pool.

!

Why it matters: if the few companies that build most templates are told to leave certain payments out, those payments are still valid and still in every mempool. They just wait for a block from someone who builds their own template. Industrial miners rarely move. At Bitcoin 2024, an IREN representative said their hash went 50/50 to Foundry and AntPool and answered "I don't know" when asked what they'd do in a 51% event. For many industrial miners, mined bitcoin is inventory to sell for power bills and debt. Home miners hold theirs, so when payments start getting left out, theirs are the ones at stake. That's why they're the ones with a reason to act.

Heat and solar

A miner turns every watt into heat and pays you back in sats. In winter that heat replaces a heater you'd run anyway. With solar, it uses power you'd otherwise sell back for a few cents. Start from a setup and adjust it to your house.

Companies building home miners

Home mining only spreads when it comes in a box a normal person can buy, install and forget about. These teams are doing that work.

Canaan Avalon

The Nano 3S, Mini 3 and Avalon Q are home miners with quiet modes and app control, from 140 W desk units up to a 90 TH/s, 1.7 kW machine.

Heatbit

Mining space heaters with air purifiers built in, up to 60 TH/s in the Maxi Pro. A miner that looks like an appliance.

Hashrate House

Immersion tanks that heat a home's hot water, reusing the old water heater as the storage tank.

Exergy

Building hashrate heating for homes and businesses, including an OCEAN and DATUM integration for Home Assistant that tracks and controls miners from your own node.

AI frees up share

When an industrial site switches to AI, its hash leaves the network and every home machine counts for more. In Morgan Stanley's middle case, about 14 of the ~20 GW of mining sites go to AI by 2028.

It doesn't fix templates

The hash that stays still mostly points at Foundry and the AntPool group. Industrial miners leaving doesn't change who builds templates for what remains.

Heat outlasts profit

Newer machines keep getting more efficient and difficulty keeps rising, so old gear earns less every year. Heat and solar are what keep a home miner worth running.

Homes needed: new home hashrate also grows the network, so its share is H ÷ (network + H). Pushing a group that builds a share c of blocks under half takes H > (2c − 1) × network. Divide by one machine's hashrate to get homes. Censored payments: if builders with share c leave a payment out, about 144 × (1 − c) blocks a day can carry it, and it waits 10 ÷ (1 − c) minutes on average. This ignores fees and assumes independent builders include every valid payment.
